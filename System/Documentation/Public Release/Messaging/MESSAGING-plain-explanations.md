## The core explanation (the generic-engine framing, literal)

"A general-purpose AI becomes more useful when it has durable context and clear procedures. Personal AI OS gives a compatible runtime your information, your rules, and your instructions in files you control. The intelligence can change; the working context stays yours."

## By question

- **What is it?** "A folder of plain text files. Some files hold what you know. Some hold rules the AI must follow. Some hold step-by-step instructions for jobs you want done. The AI reads them and works accordingly."
- **What does it do for me?** "You stop re-explaining yourself. Answers come from your own notes and decisions instead of generic averages. And the AI can safely do real tasks: file things, find things, keep it all organized, instead of just chatting."
- **How does it work?** "A supported runtime loads the standing rules, routes the request to a written procedure, and uses selected files. Supported high-risk writers show a plan, require confirmation, validate the result, and keep recovery evidence."
- **How is that different from a vendor memory feature?** "A vendor feature can be convenient, but its storage, retrieval, export, and limits belong to that product. Personal AI OS keeps its durable master in ordinary files you can inspect and move. Runtime compatibility still has to be demonstrated; the product ships profiles for Claude Code and Codex."
- **Is it safe?** "Supported retrieval filters by privacy and domain, and supported writers use review, safe paths, validation, and recovery. With direct native file access, those are compliance controls rather than a physical sandbox; the runtime can technically open files its OS account can reach."
- **Won't the AI's ideas end up in my notes?** "Not without a label. When you bring an old AI chat into the system, it counts who said what first. Your notes get built from your words. Where the AI said your idea back to you accurately, that can be kept. Where the AI added an idea of its own, it gets saved as the AI's or dropped. Nothing a chatbot said gets filed as your thinking."
- **Who's it for?** "People who repeatedly brief AI with the same context and want that context, its rules, and its procedures in inspectable files. It is especially relevant to people using filesystem-capable AI tools and those building personal automations or agents."
